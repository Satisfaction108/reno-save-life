Where do you live?

Air pollution, heat and local health trends can all affect kidney risk. Enter your ZIP code to see current conditions and CDC data for your area.

Air pollution

Fine particles from traffic and wildfire smoke get into the blood. A study of 2.5 million US veterans found more kidney disease where this pollution was higher.

Heat

Hot days make it easy to get dehydrated, and repeated dehydration can hurt the kidneys. People who work outside are most at risk.

Water

Lead from old pipes and other contaminants can harm the kidneys over time. Your water company publishes a yearly report.

Find your water report

Food nearby

Areas with more fast food and fewer grocery stores make it harder to avoid salty, processed meals.

Places to move

Without safe sidewalks or parks nearby, people tend to move less, which raises blood pressure and diabetes risk.
