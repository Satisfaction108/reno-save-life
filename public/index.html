Why kidney health matters

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) means the kidneys slowly lose their ability to filter blood. Most people have no symptoms until it's advanced, so many cases go unnoticed for years.

Bay Area

~850,000

adults in the Bay Area may have CKD. This estimate uses the national rate of 14% and the region's roughly 6 million adults.

  • ~3%of adults in each county have been told by a doctor they have it
  • 24–30%have high blood pressure
  • 10–12%have diabetes
United States

35.5M

US adults have CKD, about 1 in 7. Around 9 in 10 of them don't know it.

  • ~6%of adults aged 18 to 44 already have it
  • 2 in 3cases are caused by diabetes or high blood pressure
  • 800,000+people live with kidney failure
World

788M

adults worldwide had CKD in 2023, about twice as many as in 1990.

  • 9thleading cause of death
  • 1.48Mdeaths in 2023

Most cases aren't diagnosed

In every Bay Area county, about 3 in 100 adults say they've been diagnosed with kidney disease. Lab testing across the US finds about 14 in 100 actually have it.

Sources: CDC PLACES 2023 and CDC, Chronic Kidney Disease in the United States, 2023.
